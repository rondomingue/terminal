Welcome to Lexicon

Building your vocabulary

1Read one register above comfortable. Dense essays and older fiction surface words that frequency lists miss entirely.
2Learn roots, not isolated words. Most entries here trace a Latin or Greek root, and one root often unlocks four or five relatives.
3Use a new word within 48 hours of meeting it. Passive recognition only becomes active recall through deliberate use.
4Revisit your misses. The words you get wrong in a quiz are the highest-leverage material to restudy first.

Estimate model: your score reflects the share of a difficulty-weighted total you answered correctly, mapped onto a realistic adult vocabulary range (roughly 6,000-40,000 active words) informed by published vocabulary-size research. Passive vocabulary is projected above active vocabulary because recognition typically exceeds confident recall and use. The more words you quiz on, the more accurate the result: the 50-word quiz samples twice as many words per difficulty tier as the 25-word quiz, and the 100-word quiz samples four times as many, both reducing the effect of lucky guesses. How many times you take the quiz matters too — with a few hundred words in the bank, retaking it soon after a previous attempt raises the odds you are recognizing specific questions rather than demonstrating broader vocabulary, so a single fresh sitting is the most representative score.

This is a directional estimate, not a validated psychometric test: it samples from a curated bank of a few hundred words across five difficulty tiers, not the full dictionary. Other vocabulary tests often report a different number for the same person, partly from different sampling and partly because some count inflected word forms separately while others count word families as one entry. Treat this as a rough compass, not a certified score. Definitions are concise editorial definitions aligned to standard dictionary senses.
